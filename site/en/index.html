A free game about Peru's central bank

Sol Firme

You chair the BCR. All of Peru is watching.

Every month a card arrives: the dollar climbs, Gamarra (Lima's garment district) can't stop selling, Congress summons you. You decide what to do with the interest rate, and the month goes by. Your job is to keep inflation between 1% and 3% without switching the country off.

It runs in the browser, on a phone or a computer, by day or by night. No account, nothing to install, and from the second visit it works offline. Also in Spanish and Quechua.

How to play

One card, one decision, one month

Each decision is one month at the Board. You start with the rate only; the statement arrives in month two and the dollars in month three. What you decide today reaches prices months later.

  1. This month's card arrives

    At the top: inflation against the target, jobs and your credibility; the dollar and Congress show up when they come into play. Below: what is happening in the country.

  2. Compare and choose

    Each response says what it does with the rate, and with the dollars or the statement when they are in play, and who it hurts. Tap it and it tells you why.

  3. Predict and see what happened

    Before the result you guess whether inflation went up, stayed or went down. Then you see what you expected against what happened, and with "Tell me more", why.

Target

2% inflation, between 1% and 3%

The BCRP's target, and why protecting the value of the sol helps those who have the least the most.

Rate

The policy interest rate

Rate → loans → spending → prices

The main tool, and the road it travels until it reaches your pocket.

Lag

Deciding with an eye on what's ahead

A decision takes months to reach prices. If you wait until you see the problem, you are already late.

Word

Expectations and credibility

What the BCR says moves prices too. Breaking your word has a cost, and overreacting to a jump in food prices doesn't pay.

Schools, institutes and universities

For your economics class

Sol Firme is free for students, always. It runs on each student's phone, and an express game fits in a single class.

  • At the end of the Express Term, "What you learned" compares the entry and exit quiz and gives a code for the teacher.
  • Paste your class's codes, even a whole WhatsApp chat, and see the summary. Nothing is stored or sent.
  • Chapters with real data and their sources.
  • Works offline from the second visit, can be played without the timer and with a screen reader.
  • Workshops and a teacher's guide, in the works.

Independent projectSol Firme is not affiliated with the Central Reserve Bank of Peru (BCRP) and does not speak for it. It is an educational game: it teaches how monetary policy works and takes no side on any government or party.

Historical data comes from BCRPData, the BCRP's statistics portal. The Congress quotes are real, sourced statements shown without names; recreations are labeled. All characters are fictional. The map of Peru is based on Natural Earth, in the public domain.
